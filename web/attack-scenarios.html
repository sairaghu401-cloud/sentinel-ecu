THE EXPERIMENTS / SIX ATTACK FAMILIES

Change the conditions.
Examine the response.

Each scenario challenges a different assumption. Intensity changes the offered attack behavior; the observed evidence determines the detector response.

01 / ECU spoofing

A second source sends forged control frames using an allowed identifier. Invalid timing or counter behavior challenges the gateway even though the identifier itself is permitted.

Inspect: timing, rolling-counter and plausibility decisions for the chosen signal. Higher intensity reduces the interval between injected frames.

02 / Captured frame replay

A previously captured payload and counter are reintroduced. The gateway checks freshness using a rolling-counter baseline and the modeled time window.

Inspect: the replay reason and the prior accepted counter. This mechanism demonstrates freshness checks; it is not cryptographic authentication.

03 / CAN bus flooding

Extra traffic increases pressure on CAN A. At low intensity the offered load may remain within capacity; stronger settings can saturate the modeled segment and fill its queues.

Inspect: received, forwarded, blocked and dropped counts separately. A battery flood need not trigger a Pi payload anomaly or a critical-signal safety request.

04 / Protocol fuzzing

Generated frames use unexpected standard identifiers or malformed schema lengths. These challenge allowlist and DLC checks.

Inspect: identifier and length decisions. This laboratory supports classical standard CAN, not CAN FD, extended identifiers or arbitrary hardware fuzzing.

05 / Payload manipulation

A fraction of the target frames receive an implausible command while their normal transmission cadence is retained. Intensity sets the fraction corrupted, from 10% to 100%.

Inspect: decoded values, physical-bound violations, anomaly observations and any subsequent safety evidence. Untouched frames are not relabelled as attacks.

06 / Gradual payload drift

Valid, correctly timed payloads drift toward an altered target. Lower intensity takes longer to reach that target; higher intensity ramps faster.

Inspect: the delayed analyst observations and holds. Compare the same experiment with the analyst connected and disconnected to understand its contribution.
